Grammar Focus: -ing forms vs. Infinitives

Some verbs are followed by the -ing form (gerund), while others are followed by the infinitive (to + verb).

Verbs + -ing

  • suggest (suggest doing)
  • avoid (avoid losing)
  • risk (risk following)
  • consider (consider buying)
  • worth (it is worth trying)

Verbs + Infinitive

  • afford (afford to pay)
  • arrange (arrange to meet)
  • appear/seem (appear to be)
  • manage (manage to find)
  • In order (in order to discover)

THE AXE EFFECT

Case Study: Unilever

When Unilever wanted to launch its AXE deodorant spray for men in the United States, it combined young men's natural interest in dating with the attraction of a great house party. The idea was simple: boy buys AXE, boy meets girl, boy smells nice, girl likes boy.

The product, which was already popular in other parts of the world, was launched in the US with a powerful promotional plan to make it appeal to American male youth culture. Using the slogan 'The AXE Effect', the company used a number of marketing ploys to bring the product to the attention of the public. These included an online game, free samples given by attractive models in retail stores, point-of-sale displays, and media advertising.

All of this hyped the centerpiece of the promotion: a once-in-a-lifetime party at a Florida mansion. Unilever began by direct-mailing millions of college students and young males aged 11 to 24. A radio campaign and online publicity called for young men to log on to the Internet to play a video game on the AXE website.

Participants had to apply their dating skills to score points. If the player reached a certain level, he entered a lottery to win a trip to the party. "It was all about getting into the mind of the 20-something guy," says Mary Drapp, manager of strategic alliances.

They succeeded. Their website received more than 943,000 hits. Some 100 lucky young men were flown in to attend the party. Following the promotion, results included a 27% increase in general brand awareness among males aged 11 to 24.
